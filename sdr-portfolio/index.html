The 45-second cold call opener

Permission-based, problem-first, and ends by asking for a time. Shown here for a RevOps leader.

1 · Opener~8 sec

"Hi Jordan, it's Samuel with [Client]. I know I'm calling out of the blue. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why, and you tell me if it's worth continuing?"

2 · Problem~20 sec

"I talk to RevOps leaders at SaaS companies your size. When pricing gets more complex, quoting usually ends up in spreadsheets and Google Docs, approvals happen in Slack, and deals sit waiting at quarter-end. Does that sound like you, or do you have it nailed?"

3 · Discoverlet them talk

I ask one or two questions, then listen:

  • "Walk me through what happens when a rep needs a discount above their limit."
  • "How many tools does a quote touch before it's signed?"
  • "When did a deal last slip because of paperwork rather than the buyer?"
4 · Closefor the meeting

"Since you mentioned approvals stall at quarter-end, 20 minutes with our [Head of Sales] is worth it. They'll show how teams like yours go from quote to signed contract in one flow. Do you have your calendar handy? Tuesday at 11 or Thursday at 2 Eastern?"

Voicemail≤ 20 sec

"Jordan, Samuel at [Client]. Quick one about how your reps get discount approvals at quarter-end. I'll send a two-line email; my number's in it. Again, Samuel."

Objection handling: tap a card to flip it

My approach: acknowledge, ask a question, then reframe. I never argue. Each objection is a reason to learn more.
